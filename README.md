# About The Project
A hardware wallet exploit, and a founder saved only by rolling dice, led to an independent effort to build better Bitcoin Key Management systems. Project Astragal is a research-first project dedicated to advancing the cutting-edge of self-custody. We produce research to advance key management techniques and monitor the existing ecosystem for risks. All findings are provided open-source.

The Project is dedicated to following the research, and to building tools and projects where the research reveals gaps in the current state of the ecosystem.

We believe billions of future Bitcoin users around the world deserve the best that the community has to offer. We aim to provide it.

# Founding Principles
Open-Source
: We are dedicated to opening all our findings to the community to foster better collaboration. 

Risk-Based Approach
: We follow the research until we identify and develop risk-free solutions where possible. But technology, including Bitcoin, never exists in a vacuum. Where risk-free is not possible, we employ a risk-based approach to support better implementation of Bitcoin in the messy real world.

Process Approach
: We employ a process approach to Bitcoin key management. This enables more targeted control and solution implementation throughout the key lifecycle. The process approach also encourages stronger, well-scoped research outputs.

Narrow-Scope, Broad Evidence
: Our purpose is scoped to Bitcoin Key Management. Throughout our work, our scope is inclusive but not exclusive to Bitcoin. We will often look to other cryptographic fields as research inputs to extract learnings relevant to Bitcoin. We also take this approach to research outputs, and will scope our results broadly to cover non-bitcoin applications where appropriate. We let Bitcoin guide us, but we do not let it limit us.

Falsifiability
: We aim to remain independent and neutral. All of our claims must be scoped such that other research is welcome to prove them wrong.

# About The Name
An Astragalus is the ancient precursor to dice. The initial findings of the Project are that, even in 2026, rudimentary technology like dice are still used to reduce our trust in vendors and code.
