================================================================================
Incident Report - 2017 ROCA - Infineon RSALib
CVE-2017-15361
================================================================================

SUMMARY
Not an RNG failure. RSA primes were constructed in a fixed algebraic form as a speed optimization,
which both fingerprints the key from its public modulus and reduces the search space enough to
factor it. Included here because the affected devices were formally certified.

THE CONSTRUCTION
    [VERBATIM] Nemec, Sys, Svenda, Klinec, Matyas, CCS 2017, eq. (1)

        p = k * M + (65537^a mod M)

where M = Pn# is a primorial - the product of the first n primes - and k, a are the only unknowns.
The moduli inherit the structure:

        N = (k*M + 65537^a mod M) * (l*M + 65537^b mod M)
        N ≡ 65537^(a+b) ≡ 65537^c   (mod M)

CONSEQUENCE 1 - FINGERPRINTING
A key is identifiable as RSALib-generated if and only if the discrete logarithm log_65537(N) mod M
exists. Because M is a primorial it is smooth, so the group generated by 65537 is smooth, so
Pohlig-Hellman computes this logarithm in microseconds. The authors report no false positives across
a million tested keys - for 512-bit RSA the probability a random modulus lies in the group is about
2^-154.

An attacker can therefore scan public keys and select exactly the vulnerable ones, at negligible
cost, before spending any effort on factoring.

CONSEQUENCE 2 - ENTROPY LOSS
M is large relative to p, so k and a are small. Quoting the paper's worked example: for 512-bit RSA,
M has 219 bits, so k has 256 - 219 = 37 bits and a has 62 bits. The prime therefore carries about 99
bits of entropy rather than 256 - the pool is reduced from 2^256 to 2^99.

Factorization proceeds by searching a and applying a Coppersmith-style lattice attack for k. The
paper reports worst cases under 3 CPU-months for 1024-bit and about 100 CPU-years for 2048-bit on a
single core, with expected time half of that, and the work parallelizes.

CERTIFICATION
    [VERBATIM] from the paper's abstract:

        "The library in question is found in NIST FIPS 140-2 and CC EAL 5+ certified devices used
         for a wide range of real-world applications, including identity cards, passports, Trusted
         Platform Modules, PGP and tokens for authentication or software signing."

The researchers had access to neither source nor object code. The entire structure was recovered
from statistical properties of keys generated and exported by the certified devices themselves.
